The Humanities and Interpretive Social Sciences Research Hub brings together faculty from six departments at the University of Toronto Scarborough, so that people working on related questions in different departments find each other and work together.

## What the Hub does

The Hub builds research community across departmental lines. It runs small research clusters, a year-long series of Research Conversations, and a programme of talks, screenings and book launches, all listed on the [calendar](/events/). Its six founding departments are on the [Departments](/departments/) page.

## Research clusters

Clusters are groups of four to six faculty from at least two departments, formed around a shared scholarly interest. Each cluster writes its own working agreement and meets through the year to share work in progress, give feedback on manuscripts and grant applications, or swap teaching ideas. Facilitation rotates among members.

## Talk series

Talks, book launches, screenings and workshops from across the six departments appear on the [calendar](/events/) as they are scheduled.

*Speakers' Corner*, from the Department of Historical and Cultural Studies, is part of this series. Each Speakers' Corner talk is fifteen minutes by a faculty member on anything they think students would find worth knowing. It need not be their research: a legendary sports rivalry, a sitcom, a forgotten piece of Scarborough history, a word with an odd past. The format takes its cue from Shawkat Toorawa's [Dr. T project](https://lynnborton.com/2025/07/10/the-dr-t-project-things-worth-knowing-with-shawkat-toorawa/), whose motto is "dare to be curious".

[*Small Things*](/small-things/), the Hub's live-recorded podcast, has its own page.

## Get involved

Faculty can join a cluster, present at a Research Conversation, give a talk, or be a guest on *Small Things*. Students are welcome at every event. To suggest an event, use the "Propose an event" link on the [calendar](/events/). For anything else, see [Contact](/contact/).
